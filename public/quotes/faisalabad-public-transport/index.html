Our understanding

Six problems — and what each one costs the city

No live vehicle tracking

Every journey begins with a guess, and the authority has no independent record of where its fleet went.

Route choice is local knowledge

Usable only by people who already know the network — which suppresses ridership among everyone else.

Waiting time is uncertainty

Not knowing the headway pushes riders onto private transport and adds to road congestion.

No real-time information

Diversions and breakdowns cannot reach passengers, so one disruption strands people with no warning.

Poor network visibility

Coverage gaps stay invisible because nobody holds routes and stops as analysable spatial data.

Limited operational oversight

Planning rests on assumption rather than demand data — you cannot measure what you cannot see.

Technical feasibility

Three ways to get a position — only the first hop differs

A · Driver phone GNSS Baseline

No per-vehicle hardware or SIM. Live as fast as drivers are onboarded. Depends on the driver's device being on and in the vehicle.

B · Bluetooth / BLE proximity

Optional beacon that ties a driver's device to a specific vehicle. Not required for core live tracking.

C · Dedicated 4G tracker

For permanent, tamper-resistant tracking. Priced separately, so you can instrument part of the fleet rather than all of it.

GIS & mapping approach

Open map infrastructure we control and cache

Commercial map APIs bill per request forever. We self-host OSM-derived raster tiles and keep transport data as vector on top. Toggle the layers.

Dedicated 4G GPS/GNSS trackers, SIM/M2M connectivity and installation, where independent or tamper-resistant tracking is required. Excluded from the software cost.
